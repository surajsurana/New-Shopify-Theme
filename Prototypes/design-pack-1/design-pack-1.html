Item 2 of 5 · decision

Cap the stage, keep it left-aligned

DecisionCap. Luxury is the whole garment seen at once; a head-to-toe studio photograph with its hem cut off reads as a crop, not a couture frame. Suraj's standing rule (product photos head-to-toe, tight) is only true on screen if the hem is on screen.
CostThe image is about 18% narrower on a 1470 screen (511px to 421px). The gap to the title column widens, but the page stays calm and spacious, which suits the house. Not a downgrade in resolution: the source files are larger than either size.
RuleStage height = the lesser of 90vh and (100svh minus 200px); width follows the photograph's own ratio; never smaller than 440px wide. At 1920x1080 it resolves to about 880px (no visible change from today beyond about 8%).
MobileNot changed here. On 390x660 the sticky Appointment/Concierge bar still covers 15-19% of the hem; that belongs to the mobile PDP spec (Docs/product-page-mobile-build-spec-2026-10-21.md), where the bar can be made to collapse once the image is in view.
Optional nextTighten the breadcrumb-to-image gap (image starts at y=184 on a 99px header) by about 20px to buy back about 5% height. Not required for the cap.

Safari: this uses the same aspect-ratio plus calc family as the 19 October Safari grid bug, so the stage must be checked at 1470x771 in Safari before release.

Item 3 of 5

Journal index masthead: a face, a figure, a feeling

Before: bloom-soiree-main.jpg, a back view with the head out of frame (the template falls back to it because no image is set). After: first-love-hero.jpg (K&A's own, 1800x1350, landscape) the only landscape frame among the theme's editorial images. At 2.4:1 a portrait source can show only a third of a figure; this 4:3 original shows face, the full beaded blouse and the line of the skirt, with the focus set to 50% 6% so the head clears the header. Her face sits left of the title; the title crosses the blouse and the scrim keeps it legible. The same image already heads the First Love collection page; it is the strongest wide frame we own, and the repeat is deliberate (the Journal opens in the same warm ivory-and-red register as the collection a bride is most likely to have just seen).
