What this is

A first-purchase welcome gesture for KLP3000 (₹3,000 off), designed as a quiet invitation card — not a "discount popup" in the loud, mass-market sense. Full reasoning for placement, timing, mechanism and copy is in Docs/welcome-offer-popup-build-spec.md; this file is the working prototype referenced there.

One thing worth saying plainly up front: my standing brief for this site says "never recommend popups" — that rule exists to block the flashing-banner, countdown-timer, spin-the-wheel genre that cheapens a couture brand. Suraj asked for this one directly and explicitly, coupon and all, and asked for my judgment on how to do it without that cheapening effect. So the design below is deliberately the most restrained version of this mechanism I could build: no urgency language, no percentage badge, no gated reveal, an "invitation" register throughout, and — see Section D — it goes out of its way to stop showing itself to anyone it's already served.

B — Desktop, at rest

Centred card over a blurred/darkened scrim (42% charcoal, 3px blur — soft, not a hard blackout). 460px card, generous internal padding, a single hairline rule under the eyebrow rather than a photo — kept deliberately text-led and quiet, closer to a wax-sealed note than an ecommerce coupon tile.

C — Mobile, bottom sheet

Not a full-screen takeover. Slides up from the bottom with rounded top corners and a drag handle, leaving the page visible above the scrim — reads as a note being slipped in, not an ambush covering the whole screen. Dismiss via swipe-down, tap-outside, the × , or "Not today," any of which works with one thumb.

D — Who sees it, and how often

The card only ever fires after an eligibility check. Use the demo controls above (top of page) to actually flip your browser into each of these states and re-scroll the live section to confirm the popup correctly does — or doesn't — reappear.

Shows

First-time visitor, genuinely engaged

No prior dismiss/claim on this device, not a returning signed-in customer, and has scrolled ~60% of the current page or spent ~25s on site. Shown once for the session.

Suppressed 30 days

Dismissed ("Not today" / × / tap-outside)

One graceful re-invitation is fine; nagging isn't. Resurfaces after 30 days of no further action, then follows the same rules again.

Never again

Claimed (copied code, clicked "Begin Shopping," or sent by email)

Permanent suppression on that device. Showing a "welcome" offer to someone who already has it reads as sloppy, not enthusiastic.

Never eligible

Signed-in customer with an existing order

customer.orders_count > 0 — a first-purchase offer is never shown to someone who has already purchased. Checked server-side in Liquid before any JS trigger logic runs.

Excluded pages

Cart page

A visitor already at the cart is transacting, not browsing — interrupting that moment with an unrelated modal is the one place this would read as a hard sell rather than a welcome.

Deferred, not shown

Search overlay / mobile drawer already open

Mutually exclusive with other overlays — if one of those is open when the trigger condition is met, the welcome card waits and re-checks after it closes, rather than stacking on top.

Why not the utility bar instead?

Considered directly, since the sitewide utility bar (Complimentary Worldwide Delivery · Hand-Embroidered to Order · Private & Virtual Appointments) already sits on every page. Rejected as the primary mechanism: that bar is a fixed, evergreen set of three brand promises, already tuned to a tight three-phrase rotation with no spare room (confirmed directly in sections/ka-nav.liquid — the currency chip was deliberately kept out of it for the same crowding reason). A one-time, personal, code-bearing offer is a different kind of message than a permanent brand promise, and isn't something a visitor can meaningfully copy or act on from a marquee line. Diluting three confident, permanent promises to make room for a transient coupon message would cheapen all four.
